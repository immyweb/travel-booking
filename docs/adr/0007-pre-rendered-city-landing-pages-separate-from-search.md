# Pre-rendered city landing pages (`/[city]/stays`) are a separate route from `/search`, not a replacement

`/search` reads `searchParams` (city, country, dates, guests, amenities, page) to drive every request, which per Next.js opts the whole page into dynamic rendering — there's no way for that route to also be statically pre-generated. To satisfy the SEO requirement for pre-generated, readable-URL pages for popular searches (e.g. `https://www.travelbooking.com/london/stays`), a new route, `/[city]/stays`, is introduced instead of trying to make `/search` itself static. It renders only the default unfiltered view (no dates/guests/amenities, page 1) for a city via ISR (`generateStaticParams` + a time-based `revalidate`), with no filter bar of its own — just a link into `/search` for anyone who wants to filter. `/search` remains the single interactive, fully dynamic search experience it is today.

"Popular" is defined as "every city currently returned by `getCityCentroids`" (i.e. every city with at least one listing) rather than a curated list or search-volume analytics, since neither analytics nor a maintained city list exist anywhere in the codebase, and the city list is already small and inherently scoped to real inventory. `generateStaticParams` calls this same query at build time; a city added after the last build/revalidation still resolves on-demand (`dynamicParams` at its default) rather than 404ing.

Because the unfiltered `/search?city=X&country=Y` view and `/london/stays` render near-identical content, `/search` emits a canonical link to `/[city]/stays` (via a new `generateMetadata`) whenever no filters beyond city/country are present, to avoid duplicate-content SEO penalties — filtered variants of `/search` get no canonical override, since they're legitimately distinct content.

This is the first route in the app to use build-time static generation; every other page is SSR per [ADR-0002](0002-express-as-single-source-of-truth.md). `generateStaticParams` still fetches from Express (the same `/search/cities` endpoint the city picker already calls), so ADR-0002's single-source-of-truth boundary is preserved — the difference is only _when_ that fetch happens (build time as well as request time), not _what_ fetches it. If that build-time fetch fails, the build fails outright, the same as any other build-breaking data error, rather than silently shipping a deploy with zero pre-rendered cities.

City slugs are the city name only (lowercased, hyphenated, diacritics stripped, e.g. "São Paulo" → `sao-paulo`) — no country segment, matching the doc's own example format. Nothing in the schema prevents two different countries from sharing a city name; for v1 this is an accepted limitation (first match wins on a slug collision) rather than something solved preemptively, since today's city list has no collisions.
